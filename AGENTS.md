> Keep this file concise. Update it whenever scope or design shifts.

## What this is

Alper Ortac's personal site. A personal-brand surface for fans, community, and fellow devs — not a recruiter funnel.

## The shape

A vertical scroll journey on the main path:

- **Hero (top, sky/day)** — avatar, headline, intro.
- **Linktree** — one column, sectioned by VIDEO / POSTS / CODE.
- **Projects band** (shipped; wired between Socials and Craft) - a flat showcase of all work rendered from `src/data/projects.ts`, highlights sitting larger inside the terracotta sun-glow. Each card is a split control: the whole card dives to the project's in-site subpage, the corner pill opens its external page in a new tab. "Building now" items behave like shipped ones. Coexists with Craft's existing project triggers (both routes reach the same subpage); Craft is left untouched.
- **Craft band** — a stack of topical articles spanning the craft, tech, and personal-life threads. Each one has a heading, a one-line teaser, and 1-2 trigger cards opening a sidetrack panel.
- **Footer (ground/night)** — ambient sign-off that doubles as the Freelance / Collab contact surface: the "Let's…" headline, a message box, and a live `mailto:` "Email me" button.

**Sidetracks (deep dive subpages)** are the primary depth surface:

- Career history lives in a panel triggered from The Craft.
- Each of the four flagship projects (GoodWatch, AIStack, Forge, Manaschmiede) gets its own panel.
- Four personal-thread panels (Learning, Teaching, Family, Music) are placeholders today, reusing the Career-style yellow brutalist banner.
- A "tune the sky" panel exposes the celestial controls as a game-y find.

## Mechanics

- **Scroll** drives the day → dusk → night transition, sun arc setting, moon arc rising.
- **The scene's cast and density track progress, not only its color.** Stars thicken toward the bottom. Landscape objects vary through the day. Clouds belong to one stretch, birds to another, or both stay and their count and focus rise and fall. The sky color is one channel of the journey, not the whole of it - a section that shares its neighbour's color can still be somewhere else in the day.
- **The cast roster is locked at the member level** (ticket #58): clouds, mist, birds, fireflies, conifers - plus the existing sky, stars, sun, moon, and ridge landscape. Water was in the #58 roster but its prototype failed the walk twice (ridge-carved tarn / river seam / shore band, then a mirror lake reflecting the ladder) and ticket #83 cut it. Cut for good: water, bats, broadleaf, rocks, cabin, landmark, deer, grass, trail marks; five bird variations carry the variety instead. Per-member design runs through one look-and-feel grilling (#77) and per-member prototype tickets (#78-#83). Two placement facts hold from the sketchbooks: conifers are children of a ridge layer (depth from the ridge, planted on its crest line), and depth alone does not place a member - position relative to the crest line is its own axis.
- **The schedule model** (ticket #61): one authored table, `src/data/scene.ts`, owns every cast member's day. Each member has `windows[]` of `{start, rampIn, end, rampOut}` in global progress, plain numbers only - no member-to-member references, no anchor machinery. Presence is the max ramp value across the member's windows. The parameterizable vocabulary is **closed at five member-level scalars: presence, count, intensity, position, size**. In the table, a plain number means fixed. A track is an array of 2+ evenly spaced stops that rides the member's windowed progress piecewise-linearly - `[from, to]` is the two-stop case, `[2, 5, 3]` rises then falls (amended on ticket #63; descending and multi-stop tracks are first-class). Nothing else is schedulable - color, blur, depth, delay, and duration are not, and a member that wants a new property must amend this list first. The driver writes at most members x 5 custom properties per scroll event, per member not per element - elements fan the member value out in CSS. **Count is prefix activation**: React renders each member's max pool once (stable DOM, seeded per-element randomness by index), element `i` is active when `i < count`, and the driver flips `animation-play-state` plus a fade class only on elements whose active state changed. Presence 0 pauses the whole pool, so a scheduled-off member leaves the style budget for real (the frame-budget rule: `opacity: 0` alone buys nothing). Intensity scales motion amplitude via `calc()` in keyframes and never touches duration or delay, which stay seeded and wall-clock, so scheduled motion survives an open subpage. `scene.ts` follows the `celestial.ts` contract: the authoring panel edits session state, the file is the source of truth, a reload restores committed values.
- **Clouds and stars are on the schedule** (ticket #65, the first wired members). Clouds are #78's layered ladder: five typed pools (cirrus/mass/cumulus/twin/puff) in `CLOUDSCAPE` (`scene.ts`), each with its own window, count track, depth, and per-type alpha (translucent by design - the #78 amendment of the opaque brief; safe because every cloud window ends by 0.65, before the stars own the sky). Drift is wrap on the CSS clock (`cloud-wrap` keyframes, per-element seeded duration and negative delay), so cloud motion survives an open subpage; the driver writes only presence x alpha, count activation, one derived fill per type, and the depth-scaled day-end sink on the layer's `translate`. Cloud fill always derives from the sky at the type's depth and time (`cloudFillAt`). Stars render a 340-element pool and the driver activates a prefix: zero during the day (the whole field leaves the style budget), thickening from 150 to 340 across the night (`SCENE.stars.count`); presence stays the `starsO` ramp so the tunable sky curve keeps owning the window, and `phase2End` keeps cueing the shooting stars. The toy's `denseStars` and `forceShoot` overrides still win (`.stars-dense`, detached shoot target). Reduced motion now has its guard: twinkle and cloud drift freeze, shooting stars hide.
- **Birds are on the schedule** (ticket #64, the #80 three-species relay): far speck, swift, songbird, each a typed pool in `BIRDS` (`scene.ts`) with its own window, depth, altitude and wingbeat. Like clouds, birds live outside the member table. The relay: the speck chevron line opens at hero (progress 0, no ramp-in - it IS the hero's occasional bird) and stays to 0.46, the swift blends in for 0.10-0.40, the songbird arrives last at 0.28-0.56, small and washed far back; every window closes by 0.56, so mist owns the sunset band (stretched from the #80 seed on the walk - birds left the sky too early). Window ramps span the outer third of the window, and the layer opacity follows presence (the default fifth read as sudden on the #80 walk). A flight is a full-viewport wrapper crossing the sky on the CSS clock (`bird-cross`, seeded duration and negative delay), carrying a formation of birds; vertical paths (bob/swoop) compose inside the crossing. Direction is one per species, not per flight (the walk: mixed directions inside a type read as chaos): speck and songbird fly with the wind - the clouds' left-to-right drift, which clouds cross a step slower than birds since the same walk - and only the swift crosses against it, its sprites mirrored and the crossing reversed so it flies beak-first. Each species carries an `alpha` (presence x alpha at the layer) as the subtlety instrument, and the walk thinned the seed's flights and formations. Sprites are ASCII pixel grids compiled to crisp unit-rect paths (`gridPath`); the wingbeat is N flap beats then a glide hold, hard-cut by generated step-end keyframes (`birdFlapCss`), so all bird motion survives an open subpage. Fill derives from the sky at the species depth and time (`birdFillAt` - the sky pushed dark, washed back by distance), written once per species on the layer's `color`. The driver gates presence, live flights (prefix activation; at least one flight stays live through a ramp, the layer fade carries it) and fill. Deactivated flights fade 900ms then pause - never display:none, which pops a flock out. Reduced motion removes birds entirely (brief #77 rule 7). skyBoot seeds `--bird-<species>-o` pre-paint.
- **Mist is on the schedule** (ticket #84, the #79 interleaved veils): three groups (far/mid/near) in `MIST` (`scene.ts`), each slotted into a GAP of the ridge ladder and painted at the gap's midpoint depth (`mistGroupDepth`: 0.41 / 0.57 / 0.73, pinned by test), so the front ridge's peaks cut across the veil and it reads as hanging in that valley. Like clouds and birds, mist lives outside the member table - the first member to use `windows[]` plural: `presenceOver` takes the max across a group's windows (day plan C: far and mid carry a thin valley haze from the noon hero, all three own the sunset-to-dusk band, every window closed by 0.72 before the stars own the sky). Shape is stacked bars: the same flattened body (`VEIL_BODIES`) three times with falling fill-opacity (`VEIL_STACK`) - softness from stacking, edges pixel-crisp; the vapor exception is alpha only, and each group's alpha stays inside the brief's ~0.5 cap. Sway (`mist-sway`, alternate, seeded amplitude/period/negative delay) and the breathe opacity oscillation (`mist-breathe`, on the svg child) both run on the CSS clock, so mist motion survives an open subpage. A veil's `lift` places its bottom against its front ridge's height. The mist layer rides the SAME vertical parallax as the ridges (driver writes `translate` at the gap depth), or the veil would detach from its valley during the rise. The driver gates presence x alpha, activation, one derived fill per group (`mistFillAt` - the cloud formula pulled slightly toward the dusk silhouette) and the parallax. Deactivated veils fade 1400ms then pause, with the #64 descendant rule (`.mist-el.scene-off *`) because the svg child animates. Reduced motion freezes veils in the authored pose - vapors freeze, they are not removed. skyBoot seeds `--mist-<group>-o` pre-paint (the CW map is now `[alpha, window...]` per entry).
- **The scene-authoring panel** (ticket #63): a separate dev-only surface, not part of the sky Tune panel. Shape: a day board - one timeline row per member with draggable window bars and a playhead synced to scroll - plus per-member detail below it with numeric window inputs (start, rampIn, end, rampOut), presence previews, and the five params with a fixed/track toggle and stop add/remove. Emit is a one-shot action: a modal prints the committed `scene.ts` literal with a Copy button, values quantized to 3 decimals, board drags snap to 0.005, so the emitted file reads like a human wrote it. Gate: dev-only query param, not a game-y find, so no mobile or a11y obligations. The no-persistence rule holds: the panel edits session state, `scene.ts` is the source of truth, a reload restores committed values. Prototype: `.prototypes/scene-authoring-panel.html`.
- **Depth is one authored field, `--layer-depth`, read by two inputs on two separate CSS channels.** The dive reads it through `transform` (per-layer scale) and `filter` (blur), both gated on `body.dive-active`. Scroll parallax reads the same field through the `translate` longhand, which composes with `transform` instead of replacing it, so the dive comes out unchanged (ticket #59). `.dive-scene` already carries the subpage-scroll drift the same way. Put both on `transform` and the dive loses its zoom outright. **Blur never touches the scroll path**: blur that changes while a layer moves re-rasters every frame, and on software raster sixteen blurred full-viewport layers cut desktop scroll to 7.4fps (`docs/audits/scene-frame-budget.md`). Layers paint in depth order, not authoring order.
- **The depth ladder is capped by the art, not the frame budget.** Twenty-plus layers are affordable. The locked ladder is ten ridges spanning depth 0.21 to 0.93, and the scroll parallax is vertical only: 160px of rise at depth 1 against a 1440px reference, scaled down by viewport width. Vertical travel has a hard limit in both directions. Too much rise squeezes every ridge top onto one line and the ladder reads as one flat mass. Too much sink drops the nearest ridge past the bottom edge. Every bottom-anchored layer overscans the viewport by the full travel, or it pulls its own edge into view (ticket #59).
- **A ridge is opaque.** Delicacy comes from a fill that sits close to the sky color, not from a low opacity. A translucent ridge lets the star field read straight through the mountains. Each ridge paints the accumulated composite of every ridge behind it, which keeps the stacked wash that low alpha used to give (ticket #59).
- **The ridge ladder is shipped and replaces the single landscape silhouette** (ticket #62). `ridgeSpecs()` in `scene.ts` generates the ten silhouettes with the exact seeds walked in `.prototypes/scene-depth-parallax.html`, each a bottom-anchored `dive-layer` at its depth, so the depth sort interleaves the clouds between the mountains. Each spec carries its **crest polyline** - the placement axis for members planted on a ridge (#82's conifers). The old `--land-o` fade (0.4 to 0.2, translucent) retired with the single path: an opaque ladder cannot fade, and the day-to-night read now comes from `ridgeFillsAt` - the accumulated aerial-perspective composite against the live sky, driver-written per frame, base colour from the toy palette's landscape (through `useScrollJourney`'s `landscape`, not a component prop). The driver writes the vertical parallax on each ridge layer's `translate` longhand (`ridgeRise` x depth); reduced motion pins the rise to 0 in the driver AND `translate: none !important` in CSS. skyBoot seeds `--ridge<i>-f` pre-paint so a cold night deep-link never flashes day mountains; the port is pinned bit-for-bit in `skyBoot.test.ts`.
- **The scene keeps living behind an open subpage, and its clock is CSS, not scroll.** Opening a subpage freezes the scroll driver on purpose (`frozenRef` in `useScrollJourney.ts`, set from `panelOpenRef` in `_layout.tsx`), so the time of day cannot advance while somebody reads. Every CSS keyframe keeps running through it. `body.dive-active` adds a 3D context and per-layer zoom and blur, and none of that pauses an animation or drops the star field. Measured at 1440x900 and 390x844: twinkle and shooting-star stay `running` and advance one-to-one with the wall clock before, during and after a dive, and on a cold load straight onto a subpage URL. So any cast motion that must survive an open subpage has to come from the CSS time engine. A value the driver writes is a still frame for as long as the panel is open (ticket #60).
- **`phase2End` is load-bearing beyond the sky.** It sets full night AND cues the shooting stars: `shootO` ramps from 0 at `phase2End` to 1 at progress 1.0 (`journeyValues.ts`), so the scroll after it is their fade-in budget. Raising it shrinks that window, and at 1.0 the shooting stars never appear. The frozen background color across the last screens is deliberate - it is the still, dark backdrop the white streaks need to read against (ticket #53).
- **The sky curve is tuned as one compromise, never per device.** A section's scroll progress depends on viewport height (the Projects band centres at 0.124 on mobile and 0.178 on desktop), so one `phase1` start has to serve both. `phase1[0]` is 0.060: mobile reaches ΔE 6.0 from noon, desktop 16.9. Starting before 0.050 warms Socials past ΔE 2 and the page loses its noon top (ticket #53).
- **Vertical rhythm** is one variable: `gapVh` in `src/data/celestial.ts`, rendered as `RhythmGap`. Section roots add NO vertical padding on an edge they share with a gap, so every boundary is exactly `gapVh` (ticket #51 - ad-hoc per-section padding had made the perceived boundary run 270px to 590px). Hero's `pt-24` (nav clearance) and the footer's `pb-16` (page end) are not boundaries and stay.
- **Progress bar / minimap** always visible. Honest linear progress.
- **Sidetracks** slide in as full-screen overlay panels with a RETURN edge connector.
- **Persistence** via localStorage, for found items only. Sky tuning is deliberately NOT persisted: `src/data/celestial.ts` is the single source of truth, the dev-only Tune panel edits session state, and a reload always returns to the committed values.
- **Audio** is ambient. Default off, clear toggle.

## Constraints

- Mobile must work. Every desktop interaction needs a touch equivalent.
- Reduced-motion preference is respected.
- **A control that wraps a whole card carries an explicit `aria-label`.** Left unlabelled, its accessible name computes from everything inside it, so the screen reader's button list announces a paragraph per card instead of a name (ticket #52 measured 102 characters per Projects card). Label it with the card's title alone, and wire `aria-describedby` to the card's own description node so tabbing still gets the context. Corollary the same ticket settled: a whole-card `<button>` may not contain a heading (phrasing content only), and that is **accepted, not worked around** - heading navigation is a skip mechanism, and adjacent single-control cards have nothing to skip, so the correctly-named button list is the survey route.
